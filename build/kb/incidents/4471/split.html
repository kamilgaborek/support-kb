#4471 says one issue, one fix. It holds three problems. All three arrive under the same generic subject lines (“Date is wrong”, “Wrong day on the invite”), so the subject rule (decision 0001) merged them into one ticket. Each one now has a ticket of its own, with its count.

The parser fix closed only the first row. The form behind the second is retired. The third is still open.

* 5 of the 379 parser reports read as a one-day move. They are counted where the desk files them, under parser, and again in the one-day row.

  • parser. The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so day and month swap.
  • hint. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what it says, and the form reads it day first. The date is wrong before the parser sees it.
  • one-day. The date moves by one day, not by a swap: Tuesday becomes Monday, Lisbon and Toronto get different days for the same meeting. That looks like a time zone. The desk already noted these reports; until now they were only a footnote under the parser.

Where they ended

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October.

  • parser (#1): fixed, “fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales”. Closed as completed.
  • hint (#2): won’t fix, because the booking-v3 form is retired. Closed as not planned.
  • one-day (#3): not in the engineering update, and the parser fix does not touch a time zone shift. Still open. Send new one-day reports there, not to #4471.

How the reports were split

  • Cause by tenant, not by intake tag: the tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser, and for this ticket it is wrong often enough to matter. The rules are in causes.ts and problems.ts.
  • The one-day reports come from the desk’s notes, and their text agrees: none of them names a swapped day and month.
  • The export repeats rows across pages. Each report counts once, by id.
  • Counts follow the desk’s own view of #4471: a report counts under the cause its tenant files it under. The one-day reports are therefore in the parser count as well as in their own row.

For the desk

  • Read the body before merging when the subject is generic, like “wrong date” or “date is wrong”.
  • One cause per ticket. The same symptom can have more than one cause.
